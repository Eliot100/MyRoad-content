## Learner, start, target
- **Learner**: Adult in Israel targeting higher education who needs an English placement or exemption score.
- **Start**: After or parallel to English bagrut5; focuses on academic-style sentence completion, restatement, and reading.
- **Target**: A strong AMIRNET score for university English placement or exemption. NITE reports AMIRNET on a **50–150** scale, equivalent to the old PET English domain and to AMIR / AMIRAM. Each institution sets its own placement and exemption cutoffs; this outline does **not** invent a single cutoff or a conversion to the psychometric 200–800 score.
- **Originality note**: Samples below are fully original. No copying of NITE examples or MyRoad English content.

## Why this is not a psychometric road

From the winter (December) 2026 sitting, the Psychometric Entrance Test has only two domains: Verbal Reasoning (50%) and Quantitative Reasoning (50%). The general PET score stays on the 200–800 scale, but English no longer feeds it. English is tested only in the separate computerized AMIRNET, which institutions use for English-level placement or a waiver from English studies, unless an institution specifies otherwise. Registration for PET and AMIRNET is separate; registering for one does not register you for the other. A previous PET English score remains valid for placement or exemption for at least seven years.

### Confirmed from those pages (do not invent beyond this)

- Computerized, adaptive test of reading comprehension and vocabulary. Question types today: Sentence Completion, Restatement, and Reading Comprehension.
- About **50 minutes**, no scheduled breaks. **7 or 8 sections**: six operational sections (sentence completion, sentence completion, reading comprehension, restatement, restatement, sentence completion), plus one or two experimental sections. In the 7-section form the last section is an experimental writing task. Missing answers count as wrong; examinees may go back only within the same section.
- Scores on a **50–150** scale, equivalent to AMIR, AMIRAM, and the former PET English domain. NITE does not publish a single exemption cutoff; each academic institution decides placement, exemption, and whether the score is also used for admission.
- Scores are typically valid for placement or exemption for a **minimum of seven years**. Results appear in the NITE personal area within **10 days** and are not emailed.
- Retake wait: **at least 35 days** from the day after the previous English placement test.
- From **2027**, NITE will add sections in stages (listening comprehension and writing, and later speaking), with advance notice on the NITE site. Today's road plans the current three question types. Listening, writing, and speaking are a later schema gap, not invented here.

## Stages (estimate hours; label as estimate)

### Stage 1: Sentence Completion — Vocabulary & Context (estimate 15–22h)
- **Goal**: Fast accurate completion using academic vocabulary and collocations.
- **Topics/tiles**:
  - Understanding: Common academic words, word families, context clues.
  - Review: Timed original completions.
- **Finish criteria**: 80%+ on 20 original sentence-completion items.

### Stage 2: Restatement — Precise Meaning (estimate 12–18h)
- **Goal**: Identify which restatement preserves the exact meaning.
- **Topics/tiles**:
  - Understanding: Paraphrase techniques, negation, modifiers.
  - Review: Compare original sentence to four options.
- **Finish criteria**: Correctly choose restatement on 15 original items.

### Stage 3: Reading Comprehension — Short Academic Texts (estimate 20–28h)
- **Goal**: Answer main-idea, detail, inference, vocabulary-in-context questions under time.
- **Topics/tiles**:
  - Understanding: Passage structure, question types.
  - Review: Full short passages (original).
- **Finish criteria**: Complete 4 original passages (5 questions each) with ≥75% accuracy.

### Stage 4: Mixed Timed Practice & Strategy (estimate 12–18h)
- **Goal**: Simulate AMIRNET pacing and mixed types (adaptive sections are not reproduced; practice is mixed and timed).
- **Topics/tiles**: Review of all + strategy (guess rather than leave blank; check answers before the section ends).
- **Finish criteria**: Full mixed set under approximate time constraints.

### Stage 5: High-Level Polish (estimate 10–15h)
- **Goal**: Harder items, trap recognition, consistency.
- **Topics/tiles**: Review + difficult original items.
- **Finish criteria**: Consistent high accuracy on harder mixed set.

## Level-up rules (3 levels per stage)
- Level 1: Untimed accuracy on basic items.
- Level 2: Timed with moderate difficulty.
- Level 3: Timed hard items + explanation of wrong choices.
- Spacing: Daily short review of prior-stage vocabulary/restatement; longer passages spaced.

## 3 ORIGINAL sample items
1. **Sentence completion**: “The researchers’ findings were ______; further experiments produced almost identical results.” (options: preliminary / reproducible / contradictory / speculative)
2. **Restatement**: “Although the new policy reduced costs, it also lowered employee satisfaction.” Which restatement is closest?
   a) Costs fell and satisfaction rose.
   b) The policy cut expenses but damaged morale.
   c) Employees preferred the old policy because it cost more.
   d) Satisfaction improved once costs were controlled.
3. **Reading (short original)**:
   “A small coastal town began measuring water quality after residents reported unusual algae. Early tests showed elevated nutrient levels near the river mouth. Officials later discovered that a nearby farm had changed its fertilizer schedule. Within two seasons the algae decreased.”
   Question: What most directly explains the drop in algae? (options: new measurement tools / change in farm practice / increased tourism / colder weather)
